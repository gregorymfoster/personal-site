---
title: Three Rules for Naming Projects
date: 2024-12-03
description: My firm rules for what makes a good project or company name - two hard syllables, easy to say in a loud room, and no spelling clarification needed.
---

I've had a lot of fun naming projects silly names. In college, my later cofounder Tomas and I named our CS161 operating system "Superb Bird" after the [epically named Bird of Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogelkop_lophorina). Similarly, I named an iOS accelerometer security project "Axolotl." Later, working full-time at Airbnb, [Noah Martin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/noahsmartin) and I named a mobile push notification service "Pleiades" after [the star cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleiades). To this day, I still need to Google the spelling of that one - many a ruby bug was created due to the brutal string of vowels in that name.

Superb Bird, Axolotl, and Pleiades are all intentionally annoying (but fun names). They're hard to say and spell - even more so given I'm somewhat dyslexic.

#### Here's my firm rules for what makes a *good* project or company name

1. It must be two hard syllables
2. You can say it in a loud room without needing to repeat yourself
3. You don't need to clarify how to spell it

#### Why

1. You'll be writing and saying this name a billion times if you're lucky. Name it something straightforward. One syllable can be too indistinct, and three+ is a mouthful. Soft consonants become tongue twisters and lack punch.
2. If you're lucky, you'll pitch this name at bars, coffee shops, conferences, and a million other loud spaces. Don't sign yourself up for a decade of repeating yourself.
3. We live in the internet age. Folks will be Googling and coding this name hundreds of thousands of times. Make their lives easy.

#### Failing examples

1. Axolotl → four difficult-to-say syllables with tough spelling
2. Grindr → Someone new would need to clarify the spelling
3. Ikea → Famously needed to run a marketing campaign to teach customers pronunciation
4. Andreessen Horowitz → So many syllables, they assumed the shorter A16Z
5. Airbnb → So many publications and users still capitalize this one wrong
6. GitHub → Close, but the capitalization makes the correct spelling hard here

#### Great examples

1. Facebook, Meta
2. Sony
3. Nike
4. Apple, Macbook, iPhone
5. Uber
6. Twitter
7. Graphite (we picked it in part because it passed the three rules)
